Teacher notes

Learning goal

Children can say: the Earth leans, and it goes around the Sun once a year. When our side leans toward the Sun it is summer and hot. When our side leans away it is winter and cool. Spring and autumn come in between.

Key words

Spring, summer, autumn, winter, season, year, lean, toward, away, hot, cool.

Before the screen (5 minutes)

  1. Put a lamp on a table in the middle: "This is the Sun."
  2. Hold a ball with a sticker near the top: "This is the Earth. The sticker is Hong Kong." Tip the ball so it leans.
  3. Walk the ball slowly around the lamp. Keep it leaning toward the same wall all the way. Ask: "Is our sticker leaning toward the lamp, or away?"

On the screen (10 minutes)

  1. Adventure: ten small jobs, each worth a star. Children tap Mimi, the Sun and the Earth, fly the Earth around the Sun from season to season, choose Mimi's hat, find the friend who is having summer, and answer one picture question. Let a different child do each job.
  2. Free play: push the Earth along its path, count the years, and tap the friends to hear about each season.

After the screen (5 minutes)

Call out a season. Children mime it: fan yourself for summer, shiver and wrap a scarf for winter, smell a flower for spring, fly a kite for autumn. Then ask what they wear and eat in each season in Hong Kong.

What is simplified

  • We look at the Earth's path from the side and a little above, so the round path looks like a squashed circle. The Earth stays about the same distance from the Sun all year. The seasons do not come from being nearer or farther.
  • Seen from above the North Pole, the Earth goes around the Sun anticlockwise, as it does here.
  • The Earth also spins once every day (see the Day and Night lesson). That spin is left out here so the lean is easy to see.
  • Mimi and Sam are drawn very big, near the top and the bottom, to show the two halves. Hong Kong is really closer to the middle line than to the top.
  • Each season is shown as one quarter of the trip, with the middle of summer and winter where the lean is strongest. Real weather runs a few weeks behind, and Hong Kong's summer feels longer than its winter.
  • Hong Kong's seasons are gentle: a warm, damp spring, a hot, rainy summer, a cool, dry autumn and a cool winter with no snow.
  • The sizes and distances are not real.
